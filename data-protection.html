Backup & Disaster Recovery

Deleted files, failed systems or a disruptive attack can leave teams unable to work. Backup keeps recoverable copies; disaster recovery also plans how applications, access and dependencies will run again.

BEFORE YOU BUY

Make recovery a requirement before it becomes an emergency.

Requirements to gather

  • Inventory applications, virtual machines, databases, SaaS data and supported versions.
  • Set recovery point objectives: how much recent data could the business afford to lose?
  • Set recovery time objectives: how long could each service be unavailable?
  • Define retention, storage location, data growth, administrator access and restore-test frequency.

Define the right problem

Backup and disaster recovery restore availability. They do not substitute for DLP controls that restrict sensitive files being copied, uploaded or sent out.

COMPARE THE APPROACH

Choose by fit, existing systems and operating effort

Compare support for the same workloads and restore scenarios. Review existing hypervisors, storage, SaaS tenants and identity dependencies. Choose the delivery model and recovery destination that your team can realistically operate.

Commvault Cloud Backup & Recovery

Choose to address: Recover business workloads.

Suitable situation: Consider it for a mixed on-premises and cloud estate where supported workloads and recovery dependencies can be inventoried.

Check against existing systems: Verify application versions, hypervisors, storage and target recovery environments in the vendor support documentation.

Budget drivers: Protected data and growth, retention, storage locations, workload scope and restore testing.

Boundary: Workload coverage is version- and configuration-dependent.

Commvault SaaS Backup

Choose to address: Protect SaaS application data.

Suitable situation: Consider it for SaaS administrators who can define protected tenants, users, application data and restore permissions.

Check against existing systems: Check the specific SaaS service, tenant prerequisites and supported object types, not desktop OS compatibility alone.

Budget drivers: Protected users and applications, retained data, growth, selected service and recovery requirements.

Boundary: SaaS application APIs, permissions and object support constrain coverage and recovery.

Druva SaaS & Endpoint Backup

Choose to address: Recover SaaS and employee data.

Suitable situation: Consider it when cloud delivery fits the data-location policy and the end-user or SaaS restore requirement is clearly defined.

Check against existing systems: Check exact SaaS service and object coverage alongside endpoint OS versions and file-selection behaviour.

Budget drivers: Users or endpoints, data growth, retention, subscription scope, bandwidth and rollout work.

Boundary: SaaS and endpoint coverage require their own eligibility and entitlement checks.

Druva Hybrid & Cloud Backup

Choose to address: Protect data-center and cloud workloads.

Suitable situation: Consider it when reducing self-managed backup infrastructure is valuable and a cloud recovery design fits your workloads.

Check against existing systems: Verify hypervisor, database and public-cloud support for your exact versions and proposed recovery paths.

Budget drivers: Protected workloads, data volume and growth, retention, commitments, connectivity and restore-related charges if applicable.

Boundary: Cloud delivery does not remove workload compatibility or recovery dependency checks.

Compare licences at equivalent scope. Keep compatible systems where they meet the requirement, and use a representative pilot to verify the shortlisted configuration. Detailed capabilities and FAQs are in the linked product guides.

FEATURES, BUDGET AND SUPPORT

Compare the complete scope.

Fit with the existing environment

Bring the current product names, versions, licences and renewal dates. Check supported integrations and the effort needed to migrate, operate and maintain each option.

Size protected capacity or users, retention and growth. Include storage, cloud connectivity, recovery infrastructure, subscriptions, implementation and support. Ask which restore or consumption charges apply.

What a useful evaluation should establish

  • A successful restore of representative files and an application.
  • A documented recovery sequence with business owners and dependencies.
  • Measured recovery time and a plan for regularly repeating the test.
